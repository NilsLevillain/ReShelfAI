The other assumptions

  • Knowing a rule's euro cost is a top 3 trust driver for planners (4 of 5 explain a line unaided)
  • Non specialists can read a rule trace and its cost
  • The solver handles 2,000 products in seconds, and a what if takes under 10 s
  • Planners accept the result only if they can override it
  • No planning suite or liquidation platform explains rule cost yet
  • AI drafting helps if a human approves, and AI text can stay faithful to the numbers

Go deeper into strategystakeholders, market, pricing, risks

Who we do not serve

  • Fresh food and very short shelf life goods
  • Returns processing and warehouse resale
  • Marketplaces and auctions
  • Real time pricing and markdown engines

Stakeholders to win

  • Planning lead: likely champion of a pilot
  • Brand manager: resists clearance for premium stock until the cost is visible
  • Finance partner: cares about euros, can veto or fund
  • IT and data owner: the gate to any real data
  • Legal and procurement: can slow everything

The market in five forces

  • Buyers hold power: long cycles, in house options
  • Substitutes are strong: Excel, write off, in house teams
  • Rivalry is medium and fragmented
  • Suppliers are weak: open source solver, commodity cloud
  • Entrants are easy to build, hard to trust

Pricing detail

  • Team: about 2,000 products, 3 plants, 10 channels, 5 seats
  • Business: about 10,000 products, more plants, audit log, single sign on
  • Rejected: per seat, per solve, freemium
  • Price to find in interviews: 5 to 20% of the extra value recovered

Who else is out there

  • Optoro: returns management, no public pricing V
  • Good360: donation network, no fees stated V
  • Planning suites such as SAP IBP, Kinaxis, o9 K
  • Liquidation marketplaces such as B-Stock K

Strategic risks

  • The problem is too small, or Excel is good enough
  • A planning suite adds this as a module
  • Data is not clean enough for a pilot

What could go wrong, and the cheapest check

A red team attacked the plan. It already moved the key screen from week 6 to week 4.

Nobody cares about a rule's euro cost

The real pain may be messy data, not the rules.

So show a mock in interviews 5 to 8, three weeks before the formal test.

The comparison looks rigged

Tuned data or a weak rival make any winner meaningless.

So compare to a fair baseline, on 50 test sets chosen before tuning.

A static demo feels fake

Change a rule to a value nobody precomputed and nothing happens.

So precompute every dial position, and test it on 3 people in week 4.

Eight weeks is too short

Weeks 2 and 3 carry everything else.

So log real hours from day one, and cut scope at the week 3 checkpoint.

The rest of the red team

  • Success on seeded data can look circular: seeds are fixed and published before any tuning
  • Interviewees from one network say yes for the wrong reason: recruit outside beauty too
  • The baseline must resemble what planners really do, not a strawman
  • 8 changes to the plan came out of it, 2 already decided

Pre mortem: what blocks the launch

  • The gap over the baseline looks rigged
  • The cost of a rule cannot be priced for rules that remove options before the maths (the luxury example)
  • A static demo breaks the key flow
  • A real name or figure slips in: scan before every release
